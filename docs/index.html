I’m a sixth-year PhD student in economics at Carnegie Mellon. I am also at Revelio Labs as a data scientist. Here are some of my research interests:

  • Integrating statistical learning and decision theory with traditional economic settings, like markets and games. I especially want to understand how these settings behave with mixed populations of statistical and economic agents.

  • Using task-, worker-, firm-, and industry-level data to understand the impact of AI on (a) how individual workers do their jobs, and (b) what those jobs are.

  • Understanding the feedback and strategic considerations involved with the deployment, design, and evaluation of AI systems.

Before CMU, I was a lead dev at QuantEcon, where I created software packages like Expectations.jl and InstantiateFromURL.jl.

I’m pretty concerned about AI-generated prose seeping into our mental environments and homogenizing how we talk, write, and think. It doesn’t use commas nearly enough, for example, as it has no use for breath.

A physicist named Antoine Tilloy writes:

It is not yet clear how to balance the time spent in front of the screen, actively prompting, with the time spent pondering the output during a walk in the park, to catch up with what the dragon found

My guess at this balance is to (generally) only use LLMs on Mondays and Tuesdays. And to try to interact with LLM-generated prose as little as possible on other days. Sadly this means most of the internet is now off-limits.
